Freshman Project Written Proposal GuidelinesYour proposal should explain what you plan to do, why it matters, and how you will carry it out. Choose a focused project that you can reasonably complete within the available time. Write clearly for a reader who may be unfamiliar with your topic.Use the following sections.1 Project TitleChoose a descriptive title that communicates your project’s main idea. Include your name, course, and submission date.2 AbstractWrite one concise paragraph summarizing the problem, intended audience, proposed project, role of AI, and expected outcome. Write this section after you have developed the rest of your proposal.3 ObjectivesExplain the problem or need your project addresses and why it matters. Identify who would benefit, then list three to five specific objectives. Describe what you intend to accomplish within the scope of this project.You may explain your personal interest in the topic, but personal disclosure is not required.4 MethodologyDescribe the steps you will take to complete your project. Explain:* What you will investigate, design, create, or test.* How AI will contribute to the project.* The main features or activities you will focus on.* How you will gather feedback or evaluate your work.* How you will use what you learn to improve the final result.Include a concrete example, such as a sample interaction, use case, sketch, or planned comparison, to help readers understand your idea.5 Tools and DataIdentify the tools, information, and resources you expect to use. These might include AI platforms, software, published research, public datasets, or feedback from potential users.Explain what each resource contributes. If you have not selected a tool or secured access to data, state that clearly and explain how you will resolve it.
6 TimelineBreak the project into realistic stages. For each stage, provide a target date or week and a specific milestone.Possible stages include background research, initial design, development, testing or feedback, revision, and final presentation. Adapt these stages to your project.7 Ethical ConsiderationsDiscuss the concerns relevant to your project, such as inaccurate AI outputs, bias, privacy, accessibility, or overreliance on AI.Explain how you will address these concerns in your work. Be specific about the limits of your proposed tool or approach and where human judgment is necessary.8 Expected OutcomesState what you expect to produce by the end of the project. Possible deliverables include a prototype, concept design, analysis, sample interactions, demonstration, or presentation.Distinguish between what you will complete during the course and what might be possible with further development. Explain how you will judge whether your project meets its objectives.9 ConclusionBriefly restate the project’s purpose, its potential value, and what you hope to learn. Keep this section focused rather than repeating the full proposal.10 ReferencesCite sources used to support your background information, claims, and project decisions. Use a consistent citation style and make sure readers can locate each source.Writing Expectations* Use the section headings above to organize your proposal.* Write in complete paragraphs; use lists or tables where they improve clarity.* Explain technical terms and avoid unsupported claims.* Clearly distinguish work already completed from work you plan to do.* Keep your objectives, methods, timeline, and expected outcomes consistent.* Proofread for clarity, grammar, and unnecessary repetition.A strong proposal shows a clear purpose, a realistic plan, and thoughtful judgment about how AI fits the project.
